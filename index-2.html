A drug is a molecule. A molecule is a few electrons obeying quantum mechanics. Predicting them exactly is so hard that today we mostly guess, test, and start over.

Every electron doubles the problem.

To hold a molecule's exact quantum state, a classical computer needs memory that doubles with every added qubit. Pick a molecule.

What this does and does not show

Memory is 16 bytes per amplitude (complex double precision) times 2ⁿ. It is the worst case for exact simulation. Real chemistry software uses approximations such as density functional theory and coupled cluster, which do not store this state, but those approximations lose accuracy exactly where quantum effects are strong.

Qubit counts use the minimal STO-3G basis and the plain Jordan Wigner mapping, with no reductions. Real studies pick a smaller active space. Current quantum devices have on the order of a hundred to a thousand noisy qubits, and noise rather than qubit count is the main obstacle.

Classical simulation

Solves the physics equations on ordinary computers.

Strong at small molecules, where it is exact and trusted.

Struggles with large ones. Cost explodes, so approximations take over.

Machine learning

Learns patterns from data. Transformer models such as AlphaFold predict protein structures.

Strong at speed. It already works at scale.

Struggles with problems with little data. It predicts, it does not compute the physics.

Quantum simulation

Stores a molecule's quantum state directly in qubits.

Strong at scaling with the physics, with no training data.

Struggles with today's hardware, which is small and noisy.

These are teammates, not rivals. The method in this project, VQE, is itself a pair: a quantum circuit proposes a state and a classical optimizer improves it. A quantum computer does not try every answer at once. It arranges the problem so the physics does part of the work.

What is H₂, and why start here?

H₂ has two electrons and is the simplest molecule that shows real quantum chemistry. It fits on two qubits and a classical computer can solve it exactly, so every VQE answer can be checked. That is why it is the standard test case in the VQE literature.

It is a benchmark for the method, not a drug. The biomedical link is that larger molecules need the same calculation.

How these numbers were made
  1. PySCF computes the electron integrals for H₂ (STO-3G basis) at each distance, plus the exact full CI energy as a reference.
  2. OpenFermion turns them into a qubit Hamiltonian and reduces it from 4 qubits to 2 using symmetries. The constant term includes nuclear repulsion, so energies are totals in Hartree. The lowest eigenvalue matches the PySCF reference at all 45 distances.
  3. A 4 parameter circuit (RY, RY, CNOT, RY, RY) was optimized with COBYLA on the Amazon Braket local simulator, starting near the Hartree Fock state. The worst error across 45 distances was – Ha.
  4. The lab above runs the same circuit in your browser with a 2 qubit simulator and gradient descent (Adam with the parameter shift rule). It was checked against the Braket results at every distance.

All runs are simulations. None ran on quantum hardware.

Where it could help, and where it cannot.

Developing a drug often takes a decade or more, and much of that is clinical testing on people. Quantum computing is aimed at the early stages. Tap a stage.

Five areas. Five maturity levels.

Transformers and quantum computers.

The 2017 paper Attention Is All You Need introduced the transformer behind modern language models and AlphaFold 2. It is a different kind of thing from a quantum computer, so this is about roles, not a winner.

The honest part.

Few, noisy qubits.

Demonstrations stay near two to a few dozen qubits, far from drug sized molecules or whole proteins.

Short circuits only.

Gate errors and decoherence limit how deep a circuit can run before results fall apart.

No error correction yet.

Fault tolerant machines that could run longer algorithms are still in development.

No proven advantage.

Review papers broadly agree that a clear quantum advantage on a practical biomedical task has not been shown.
